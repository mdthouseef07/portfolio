About

Most infotainment bugs don't show up in a demo — they show up three months later, in the cold, on a low battery. Finding those before a vehicle ships is what I do. I design automation frameworks in Python, C, Robot Framework, and Appium, wire them into Jenkins/GitLab CI, and get close to the hardware — CAN, CAN FD, UDS (ISO 14229), SPI, I2C, UART — with CANoe, oscilloscopes, and logic analyzers to run the checks a demo never catches. That work has shipped across three OEM programs and earned three Visteon Silver Awards.

Why hire me

Straight answers

What kind of role are you looking for next?
Test automation or embedded validation roles in automotive or embedded systems — ideally in Singapore or Europe. I'm also actively growing toward firmware and embedded software development, so roles with room to move in that direction are a strong fit.
Why the shift from validation toward firmware development?
Automation and validation taught me exactly how firmware fails in the field — timing bugs, protocol edge cases, power-mode issues. I want to build the firmware, not just test it, so I've been putting in the STM32 and RTOS work outside my day job to make that move.
What should a hiring manager know that isn't on the resume?
I build things that outlast the program they were built for. The CI/CD frameworks and regression suites I've put together at Visteon are still catching defects on every build, long after I moved on to the next feature.
